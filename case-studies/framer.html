Framer: Enhancing Academic Market Adoption through UX & Student Research

As Framer positions itself as a best-in-class design and prototyping platform, this project explores how college students — particularly those studying design and computer science — currently perceive and engage with it, in order to unlock deeper academic adoption.

Opportunity Statement

As Framer continues to position itself as a best-in-class design and prototyping platform, there is a growing opportunity to better serve and expand its presence among college students — particularly those pursuing design and computer science. This project explores how student users currently perceive and engage with Framer, identifying usability pain points, adoption blockers, and opportunities to increase academic stickiness and trust.

Research Focus

Perception & Awareness

How students currently discover, evaluate, and decide whether to adopt Framer over incumbent tools they already know from class.

Usability Blockers

Where the learning curve, pricing model, or feature set creates friction specifically for a student — not a professional — workflow.

Trust & Stickiness

What would make Framer a student's default tool for class projects and portfolios, not just a one-off trial.

Approach

  • Interviewed design and computer science students to map current tool preferences and workflows (e.g. Figma, Webflow, and course-mandated tools).
  • Evaluated Framer's onboarding and learning curve specifically through a first-time student lens.
  • Benchmarked Framer's academic positioning and pricing against tools already embedded in university curricula.
  • Synthesized findings into UX and go-to-market recommendations aimed at increasing trust and day-to-day stickiness in academic settings.
