Product Strategy

Depop: Improving Convenience & Reliability in the Buyer Experience

Goal: determine a product strategy to ensure convenience and reliability in Depop's buyer experience — from search and discovery through checkout.

Company

Depop — peer-to-peer resale marketplace

Users

~30M platform users

Method

User interviews + competitive analysis

Benchmarked

Grailed, eBay, Poshmark, Pinterest

Background

Depop is a resale-and-social e-commerce platform where sellers and buyers can DM one another, like items, and follow one another. It's rooted in vintage and secondhand fashion, and hosts around 30 million users who primarily buy and sell clothing, accessories, and a smaller selection of art, homeware, and records.

Executive Summary

Pain Point 1: Discovery

Users struggle to find items when they don't know the exact name or brand of a piece, or when they want to shop an entire aesthetic rather than a specific keyword.

Solution: reverse image search, plus recommendations curated from Pinterest-board-style inputs.

Impact: ↑ discovery → ↑ listing views → ↑ purchases.

Pain Point 2: Pricing Autonomy

Buyers are less inclined to purchase because they have no autonomy over pricing, especially when trying to buy multiple items at once.

Solution: let buyers make a single offer across 2+ items automatically (bundle offers).

Impact: ↑ buyer initiative → ↓ abandoned carts → ↑ purchases.

User Research

Interviews surfaced four recurring experience pitfalls, weighted here by how often they came up.

0 said search lacks depth — hard to discover new items
0 found bundling tedious without a way to make offers
0 cited inaccurate/absent listing details from lax seller requirements

Persona: Tim, the intentional secondhand shopper

Tim wants to buy a winter coat — black, warm, and trendy — but isn't sure of the exact model or design. He starts with a broad search ("black winter jacket") and refines it by scrolling and spotting details like "lobster clasps." Without image search or brand verification, it's difficult for him to sift quality listings from mediocre ones, and often no sale goes through at all.

What competitors do well

Grailed offers "Grailed Verified" tags for reviewed items. eBay lets buyers filter for past sales to understand going prices. Poshmark has strong native bundle-offer features. Pinterest, while not a direct competitor, has a reverse-search experience that outperforms Depop's keyword-only search.

Feature Prioritization

Pain PointFeaturePriorityReasoning
Can't find items without the right keywords Reverse image search + Pinterest integration High Improves discoverability and search satisfaction; medium/high engineering effort, but highest impact on engagement.
Uncertainty in sizing & authenticity Required measurements, tag scan, verification filter Med/High Builds trust and reduces returns; high effort for backend verification, but trust outweighs the listing delay.
Can't negotiate prices on bundles "Make Offer" on bundle deals Med/High Drives multi-item purchases and higher cart value with moderate UI/backend effort.
Limited filtering options Additional filters Low Improves efficiency but deprioritized in favor of trust and discovery fixes.

Implementation Timeline

Q1 · Jan–Mar

User research & interviews; begin reverse image search + Pinterest integration build.

Q2 · Apr–Jun

Build UI/UX for bundle deals; continue core feature development.

Q3 · Jul–Sep

Testing & feedback across both features; collect usage data.

Q4 · Oct–Dec

Refine UI, add features based on feedback, track year-end KPIs.

Target KPIs

Product Discovery

30% of users adopting reverse image search · 10% increase in search-to-transaction conversion · 20% reduction in no-result searches.

Purchase Behavior

10% increase in average order value · 30% bundle-offer acceptance rate · 40% increase in multi-item purchases.
